\begin{abstract}
WildFront BattleGrounds (v3.4.1) is an open-source turn-based hexagonal tactical strategy game written in Rust featuring symmetric procedural arena synthesis, a 152-card deck builder, an expected-value AI tactician, lockstep TCP multiplayer, a built-in 3D-print .STL Export Studio (F8), and a custom 64-tree 3D voxel raymarching graphics engine. Set in 2072 after nuclear conflict and climate collapse drove humanity into underground bunkers with genetically engineered organisms, the game uses procedural hex geometry to guarantee an equal playing field for both players across 127- to 217-tile arenas (160 symmetry checks and 732 path checks). Built-in quantitative game balancing audits all 152 tactical cards (cost-utility Pearson r = 0.998889 across 102 Veteran AI matches), deterministic 64-bit FNV-1a hashing keeps 237 turn transitions bit-identical, hierarchical 64-tree voxel compression packs 170 3D models by 8.89x into 47.71 MB (268,976 CPU debug rays/s), and wf\_assets exports manifold binary .stl miniatures and hex terrain tiles for physical tabletop 3D printing.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Setting and Tactical Foundations: " "The year is 2072. Only six continents remain. The earth has been wracked by global warming, drought, and radiation following years of nuclear war. Humanity has retreated into underground bunkers along with a handful of surviving plants and animals. Lacking the resources to construct new military equipment, each surviving group of humans sought to bolster their forces through genetic experimentation. Armed with the most lethal lifeforms across the planet, humanity has begun to emerge from their underground dwellings. The war on the surface, the Wildfront, is where you stand today." " Turning a tactical world into a fair competitive strategy game requires rigorous discrete geometry, built-in quantitative game balancing, and efficient 3D rendering. Whenever a strategy game synthesizes random arenas instead of reusing fixed static maps, procedural symmetry is what guarantees an equal playing field for both players. Without verified rotational and mirror equivalence on axial hex coordinates, a sheltered ridge positioned one tile closer to player one's deployment pad---or defensive cover clustering along a single flank---skews win probabilities before the opening turn begins. Built-in quantitative game balancing addresses the parallel challenge across the 152-card tactical roster: auditing card deployment costs against measured combat utility across round-robin AI tournaments ensures cheap reconnaissance units, artillery specialists, and heavy organisms compete fairly without collapsing deck variety into one dominant meta list. Sparse Voxel Compression, 3D-Print Export, and Lockstep Multiplayer: Real-time miniature rendering and multiplayer determinism impose matching engineering discipline. Storing sculpted 3D unit miniatures as dense arrays wastes memory on empty air; hierarchical 64-tree voxel buffers pack away empty space cleanly [1] while also serving as direct geometric source meshes for the built-in .STL 3D-Print Export Studio so players can print physical game pieces and interlocking hex tiles. Meanwhile, 64-bit FNV-1a state hashing guarantees two remote computers stay bit-identical over peer-to-peer TCP lockstep matches [2] .

\section{Architecture \& Methods}
Four Modular Crates: Rules, Assets \& .STL Export, AI Balance, and GPU Client: WildFront BattleGrounds (v 3.4.1 ) separates deterministic rules, asset pipelines, AI balance tournaments, and the 3D client across four Rust crates so live matches and batch simulations obey identical logic. In wf\_core , hex\_map.rs manages axial hexagonal coordinates while mapgen.rs constructs symmetric hills, defensive cover, bridges, and objective pads ensuring equal playing conditions for both sides. Turn phases, Fog-of-War line of sight, combat, traits, and deck rules reside in combat.rs , abilities.rs , and logistics.rs . On the 3D asset pipeline, wf\_core/voxel.rs and wf\_assets/vox\_baker.rs pack .vox models into 64-tree sparse voxel buffers [1] , while wf\_assets/stl\_exporter.rs converts occupied voxel faces directly into watertight manifold binary .stl meshes ready for physical tabletop 3D printing. AI Game Balancing, Lockstep Hashes, and the Interactive Studio (F8 .STL Export): In wf\_sim , sim\_engine.rs runs deterministic match loops and computes 64-bit FNV-1a replay hashes [2] ; ai\_planner.rs evaluates expected positional value; and tournament.rs provides built-in quantitative game balancing across automated leagues. Inside wf\_client , a custom GPU raymarcher ( voxel\_pipeline.rs ), spatialized sound ( audio.rs ), a 152-card Deck Builder, a Map Studio, TCP lockstep networking, and the press- F8 3D-Print .STL Export Studio let players export miniatures and terrain tiles cleanly offline.

\section{Capabilities \& Benchmark Results}
Symmetric Maps, Lockstep Replays, and 152-Card Game Balance: Evaluating procedural battlefield synthesis across five representative arena layouts ranging smoothly from 127 up to 217 hex tiles demonstrates strict fairness: WildFront BattleGrounds honored all 160 exact reflection and rotational symmetry invariants alongside 732 terrain navigability checks guaranteeing every unit deployment zone connects smoothly to every map objective without trapped valleys. Advancing three complete Veteran-AI battles through 237 player turns reproduced bit-identical 64-bit FNV-1a digests cleanly across replays ( 0x502ecab43d99b1fd , 0xb27519b6313e86df , and 0xd27c65a5437a354c ) [2] ---proving network opponents remain locked byte-for-byte in step by transmitting tiny move commands alone. Auditing competitive balance across all 152 playable cards confirms disciplined cost-to-value calibration: deployment command costs track measured tactical combat utility closely at Pearson correlation r = 0.998889. Across 102 automated Veteran-AI round-robin matches generating 82 decisive finishes at a brisk 14-turn median match duration, six hybrid multi-faction decks spanned competitive 30.0\% to 80.0\% win shares while nine focused single-faction lists spanned 12.50\% to 56.25\%, logged transparently inside faction\_balance\_matrix.csv . 8.89x 64-Tree Voxel Compression and Raymarching Throughput: Benchmarking graphics efficiency across all 170 unit miniatures, obstacle props, and terrain voxel rigs proves crisp hierarchical compression: baking condensed 423.96 million dense cubic voxels (containing 10.04 million solid occupied material voxels) down into a compact 47.71 MB 64-tree buffer---delivering 8.89x GPU memory compression while tracing 268,976 CPU debug rays per second cleanly even before parallel hardware shader acceleration [1] . Looking inside those numbers clarifies why craftspeople love sparse voxel engines and game-theory audits alike. Imagine building tiny Lego tank and infantry miniatures inside invisible shoeboxes made of air: storing every empty air cube inside a giant 3D grid wastes nearly ninety percent of graphics memory describing nothing at all. A 64-tree divides space into neat four-by-four-by-four sub-cubes recursively---skipping empty rooms completely with a single zero bit while storing solid colored armor and terrain tiles tightly at leaf depths, letting raymarching beams step briskly across battlefields [1] . Over on the tactical ruleset side, why does checking 160 mirror and rotational symmetries plus 732 route evaluations matter for players? Because procedural generators feel thrilling only until a player opens a match and sees an unbeatable mountain ridge blocking one side's supply road. Verifying geometric invariance alongside connectivity guarantees every player commands an honest, balanced arena on every random seed while still enjoying novel choke hills and forest clusters every skirmish. Meanwhile, evaluating 152 tactical cards across 102 automated Veteran-AI matches converts deck design from trial-and-error guesswork into playful empirical science. A cost-to-utility correlation of r = 0.998889 confirms that cheap scout squads, mid-tier artillery, and expensive heavy walkers deliver proportional power per deployment resource spent---ensuring neither pure-faction lists (12.50\% to 56.25\% wins) nor creative hybrid decks (30.0\% to 80.0\% wins) crowd out thoughtful play over engaging 14-turn medians. Finally, stamping exact 64-bit FNV-1a replay hashes at every turn end lets two remote laptops play seamless lockstep matches over basic home connections without ever streaming heavy 3D physics frames [2] .

\section{Applications \& Use Cases}
Strategy Players, Deck Builders, and Tabletop 3D Printing: Tactics enthusiasts seeking turn-based hexagonal gameplay free of microtransactions or external matchmaking servers can launch offline skirmishes against Veteran AI immediately, invite friends across lockstep TCP sessions, customize decks across all 152 cards inside the visual Deck Builder, design balanced maps in Map Studio, and open the built-in 3D-Print .STL Export Studio ( F8 ) to export manifold binary .stl creature miniatures and interlocking hex terrain tiles for physical tabletop play. Computer Graphics Learners and Game-AI Tinkerers: Graphics students, discrete-math teachers, and hobbyist AI programmers equally gain a crisp playable reference linking pure axial hex symmetry, FNV-1a lockstep determinism [2] , 64-tree wgpu raymarching [1] , and auditable faction\_balance\_matrix.csv tournament statistics across straightforward Rust crates. Balanced Turn-Based Strategy Without Endless Patch Guesswork: Tabletop board-game designers, campus strategy clubs, and independent game creators wrestle constantly with subtle rule loopholes and runaway card combinations that only appear after hundreds of careful matches. In many commercial releases, balance adjustments remain hidden behind server-side patches that leave players guessing why one faction felt unstoppable on certain maps. WildFront BattleGrounds takes an open, transparent path: hexagonal movement ranges, elevated terrain lines of sight, and deterministic combat resolution allow creators and curious players to examine hundreds of simulated AI skirmishes straight from plain spreadsheet outputs, clarifying whether an opening tactic shines because of genuine positional skill or unintended deck imbalance. Open Craftsmanship in Voxel Engineering and Mathematical Game Design: For general technology readers and aspiring software builders, WildFront BattleGrounds also serves as an approachable window into ideas usually concealed inside massive multi-gigabyte commercial game engines. Storing three-dimensional canyon blocks inside compact 64-tree hierarchies keeps terrain rendering crisp on ordinary laptops, deterministic lockstep synchronization guarantees two distant opponents observe the exact same tactical turn without central matchmaking servers, and spatial game-theory tradeoffs make every elevation advantage immediately legible. Listed within the public catalog at crvscience.com/apps alongside physics, chemistry, and environmental applications, WildFront BattleGrounds invites both casual tacticians and curious learners to explore combinatorial strategy and clean voxel geometry directly on desktop hardware. Interactive Math Clubs, Map Creators, and Preservation Without Servers: High-school math circles, weekend board-game meetups, and archival gaming enthusiasts often notice a frustrating pattern across modern digital games: once a publisher turns off cloud authentication servers, thoughtfully crafted tactical arenas disappear overnight. Because WildFront BattleGrounds keeps deck builders, voxel Map Studio canvases, Veteran AI opponents, and direct peer-to-peer lockstep sessions completely self-contained offline, local communities own both their custom islands and match histories indefinitely. Instructors introducing discrete probability or minimax lookahead can even showcase how subtle terrain chokepoints swing strategic resource curves---turning recreational play into lively shared curiosity about graph paths, geometric symmetry, and fair game design. Whether players enjoy slow tactical skirmishes beside friends or dissect automated deck-balance curves over afternoon coffee, WildFront BattleGrounds treats player intelligence respectfully with transparent rules and crisp voxel craftsmanship.
